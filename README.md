# Introduction: Sources and Evolution of Islamic Jurisprudence

Islamic Fiqh (الفقه) is a living science. It is not merely a collection of legal rulings, but a divinely guided intellectual framework that harmonizes revelation with human reasoning.

## Primary Sources of Islamic Law

```text
The Noble Qur’an
        ↓
The Prophetic Sunnah
        ↓
Consensus of the Companions (Ijma‘)
        ↓
Analogical Reasoning (Qiyas)
        ↓
Independent Juristic Reasoning (Ijtihad)
```

---

# Chapter 1: The Prophetic ﷺ Methodology of Thought and Problem Solving

## 1.1 Foundation Principle: Dependence on Revelation

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ never issued religious rulings based on personal desire. He waited for revelation and divine guidance.

### Qur’anic Evidence

> “Nor does he speak from his own desire.  
> It is nothing except revelation revealed.”
> — Surah An-Najm 53:3–4

### Historical Practice

Whenever difficult questions were asked and revelation had not yet arrived, the Prophet ﷺ would say:

> “I do not know until revelation comes to me.”

This demonstrates intellectual honesty, humility, and prophetic integrity.

---

## 1.2 The Five-Layer Prophetic Problem-Solving Model

```text
Step 1 → Is there a direct Qur’anic ruling?
        ↓
Step 2 → Derive principles from previous revelation
        ↓
Step 3 → Consider Maqasid al-Shari‘ah
        ↓
Step 4 → Evaluate social context and customary practice (‘Urf)
        ↓
Step 5 → Consultation (Shura) with the Companions
```

---

## 1.3 Maqasid al-Shari‘ah (Objectives of Islamic Law)

The Prophet ﷺ consistently protected five universal necessities:

| Objective | Arabic Term | Purpose |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Preservation of Religion | حفظ الدين | Protect faith |
| Preservation of Life | حفظ النفس | Protect human life |
| Preservation of Intellect | حفظ العقل | Protect reasoning and sanity |
| Preservation of Lineage | حفظ النسل | Protect family and lineage |
| Preservation of Wealth | حفظ المال | Protect property and economics |

---

## 1.4 Prophetic Tafsir Methodology

### A. Qur’an Explains Qur’an

One verse is interpreted through another verse.

### Example

The word “zulm” in Surah Al-An‘am 6:82 was explained by the Prophet ﷺ using Surah Luqman 31:13, clarifying that “zulm” here means shirk.

---

### B. Sunnah Explains the Qur’an

The Qur’an commands prayer, but the Prophet ﷺ demonstrated its practical method.

> “Pray as you have seen me praying.”
> — Sahih al-Bukhari

---

### C. Legislative Clarification (Bayan)

Three types:

```text
1. Bayan al-Taqrir → Confirming meaning
2. Bayan al-Tafsir → Detailed explanation
3. Bayan al-Tashri‘ → Establishing new legal rulings
```

---

## 1.5 Key Characteristics of Prophetic Legal Reasoning

### Context-Based Answers

The Prophet ﷺ gave different answers to different individuals based on their personal circumstances.

### Gradual Legislation (Tadarruj)

Example: Alcohol prohibition occurred in stages.

```text
Stage 1 → Recognition of benefit and harm
Stage 2 → No prayer while intoxicated
Stage 3 → Complete prohibition
Stage 4 → Social enforcement
```

### Ease and Removal of Hardship

> “Make things easy and do not make them difficult.”

### Blocking Harmful Means (Sadd al-Dhara’i)

Preventing future corruption even before it appears.

# Chapter 2: Methodology of the Companions (Sahabah)

## 2.1 Foundations of Sahabi Ijtihad

The Companions learned not only rulings from the Prophet ﷺ, but also his methodology of thinking.

### Mu‘adh ibn Jabal Framework

```text
1. Qur’an
2. Sunnah
3. Personal Ijtihad
```

---

## 2.2 Types of Sahabi Ijtihad

### A. Text-Based Ijtihad

Direct derivation from Qur’an and Sunnah.

### Example

Abu Bakr (RA) fought those who rejected Zakat because Salah and Zakat are consistently paired together in the Qur’an.

---

### B. Maslahah-Based Ijtihad

Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) often considered public welfare and long-term consequences.

#### Examples

| Decision | Principle |
|----------|------------|
| Compilation of the Qur’an | Preservation of religion |
| Organizing Tarawih in congregation | Public benefit |
| Suspension of Mu’allafah funds | Changed circumstances |
| Non-division of conquered lands | Future generational welfare |

---

### C. Qiyas-Based Ijtihad

Ali (RA) analogically extended punishment for intoxication due to its connection with false accusation and harmful speech.

---

## 2.3 Causes of Scholarly Differences Among Sahabah

### Main Reasons

```text
1. A hadith did not reach all Companions
2. Different interpretations of texts
3. Lack of knowledge about abrogation (Naskh)
4. Different contextual perspectives
```

# Chapter 3: The Four Major Imams and Their Methodologies

---

# 3.1 Imam Abu Hanifah (RAH)

## Core Methodology

```text
1. Qur’an
2. Sunnah
3. Consensus of Companions
4. Statements of Companions
5. Qiyas
6. Istihsan
7. Custom (‘Urf)
```

## Distinctive Contribution: Istihsan

Definition:

> Departing from strict analogy when a stronger public or practical necessity exists.

### Examples

- Leasing contracts (Ijarah)
- Manufacturing contracts (Istisna‘)

---

# 3.2 Imam Malik (RAH)

## Core Methodology

```text
1. Qur’an
2. Sunnah
3. Practice of the People of Madinah
4. Ijma‘
5. Fatwas of Companions
6. Qiyas
7. Maslahah Mursalah
8. Sadd al-Dhara’i
9. Istishab
```

## Distinctive Contribution

### ‘Amal Ahl al-Madinah

The living practice of Madinah was considered a preserved form of Sunnah.

---

# 3.3 Imam al-Shafi‘i (RAH)

## Major Contribution

Founder of Usul al-Fiqh as a formal science through his book:

> Al-Risalah

## Core Methodology

```text
1. Qur’an and Sunnah
2. Ijma‘
3. Statements of Companions
4. Qiyas
```

## Famous Principle

> “If the hadith is authentic, then that is my madhhab.”

---

# 3.4 Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal (RAH)

## Core Methodology

```text
1. Qur’an and authentic Hadith
2. Fatwas of Companions
3. Weak Hadith (before Qiyas)
4. Qiyas when necessary
```

## Distinctive Feature

He preferred weak hadith over speculative analogy if fabrication was absent.

# Chapter 5: AI Agent Fiqh Decision Workflow

## Islamic AI Fatwa Decision Flowchart

```text

        ↓

    ├─ Religion
    ├─ Life
    ├─ Intellect
    ├─ Lineage

        ↓

    ├─ Social customs (‘Urf)
    ├─ Harm prevention
    ├─ Ease and hardship
    └─ Long-term consequences
        ↓

    ├─ Evidence-based ruling
```
